For the past 10 days I've been deeply practicing photography.
I joined this workshop where we travel across India, which is a completely new environment for me, new people.
And we were practicing street photography and just event photography.

I'm at a point where I want to tell better stories.
I want to be more intentional with shots and what I do.
This workshop was perfect for getting me to do all of that.

## Chennai: Ordinary Moments

The story itself is we traveled first to Chennai and got a taste of what street photography is.
Honestly we started with ordinary stuff.
The point is there's stories all around us.
We went to a bazaar where it's just people doing random stuff.
Then the next day in Chennai we went to a fish market, a flower market, and then we went to a beach.
It's all random ordinary stuff, but yet there were stories, and I had to be like, okay, we're not really achieving anything, we're just walking around observing the world.
That was weird to me because all this time my sense was you have to be doing something.
That's how all my stories came about.

## Thiruchendur: Overwhelmed by Chaos

Next we went to Thiruchendur.
It was like a long trek because we had to fly to Kerala and then take a four-hour uncomfortable bus ride.
It was hard.
And once we got there, we went to this Dashera festival, this Kulasai festival where people from across Tamil Nadu leave their villages dressed up as these deities and gods and come celebrate Dashera.
Basically at the end of the day they march to this beach and do these rituals and it's insane.

The Kulasai festival was freaking chaos.
So many photographers going into these intimate rituals and moments, just to take pictures.
They weren't living in the moment, they were just trying to document it.
That was pretty against my instincts because whenever I go to a place, I want to just observe.
But these guys were like paparazzi shooting.

At a certain point I realized, I came here with a purpose.
Just get my shots.

My task was kind of hard at first because like, all these people are doing this cool stuff but I don't know where it starts.
Overwhelmed.
I barely still knew how to use my camera and everything.
And that was a couple of brutal days because we all bonded over starving and the bus.

It was especially interesting because I actually went to Thiruchendur a couple weeks prior.
I mean I just went to a single temple, but this was like a completely new side of this whole area.
We didn't even go to that temple I went to first and there was still so much to do, so much happening.
It was fascinating that there's so many different stories in such a small area.
So many sides to life that you would never even experience.

## Varanasi: The Breakthrough

After that we flew back and went to Varanasi.
Now I finally started getting good at photography.
Getting good as in being more observant to small details.
Now I was comfortable with not having to achieve anything but literally just playing around, looking around my surroundings.
And it was crazy how much color there was.
A lot of red, orange, blue.
Everything was vibrant.
It just symbolized energy.

Seeing all the ghats built on the river, how long the they stretched, what history they held.
How did they even build up all these different steps on the Ganga?
Such an insane construction project.
It was fascinating seeing how it all evolved.
At first we went to the Ganga in the morning and we saw people doing these rituals and all these different things.
And then we went back home for a bit and got some food.
Then at night we went to this aarti.

This was when it clicked.
I took a picture of this lady holding a lamp, super up close.
It looked almost ethereal.
There was background stuff happening, but this small moment—it was everything.

Before this, I was taking pictures of landscapes, the whole scene.
It was cool, but generic.
Getting forced to go up close made me focus on framing.
What's important and what's not.
The perspective.
The leading lines up to the subject.

My challenges were capturing people's hands, small moments.
I had to be more observant of the environment.
Up until then, I felt like this whole workshop wasn't going to do anything.
But at the aarti, it was like, wait, there's specific stuff happening here.

I had to frame better.
It's a lot I had to learn.
It was hard, I felt so bad, but yet I was pushed, pushed, pushed.

Part of feeling bad was getting close to people.
Part of it was, "Am I learning fast enough?"
"Why don't I have a good eye?"

But now, after the experience, I'm fine.
I'm at my skill level for a reason.
I don't feel bad anymore.

## Kolkata: Dialing It In

Next day we went on a boat tour and we went around the alleys of Varanasi and I was getting even better at framing, noticing the small things.
I also started to realize that me carrying a bag was too cumbersome.
I had to just drop it.
Finally, we went to Kolkata.
Now Kolkata was where my training ramped up.
Now I even tried experimenting with Leica.
I was slowly starting to master the fundamentals on my camera, but now it's like, just stick to raw ISO, aperture, and shutter speed.
I had to frame a lot better.
I had to catch focus a lot better.
I had to go in closer and closer to faces.
I had to find real stories.
I had to find multiple layers packed with foregrounds and backgrounds.
I'd already started to learn about it, but now it's like I have to dial it in.
I had to look for cool little colors and pay attention to where the light is.

## 10 Days, Gone

Now it's the last day and like every single experience I've been on, it is insane how fast it has all passed.
10 days in a snap of the fingers.
It's sad to see these guys already gone when we're just meeting them a couple days ago.
Thankfully, I know for a fact I will stay in contact with all these people, but it's like, wow, how fast does time fly?

## The Mentors

I met Gajin, the main mentor, a couple months ago on this Antarctic cruise.
He's a photographer who shot for Leica, and we connected there.
He mentioned he was doing this India workshop, and I just took a chance and joined.

Gajin brought in Shivam, a fixer who knows India well.
There was also Kevin, another student who's already a pretty good photographer, especially with camera settings.
And Jacob, the other student who joined.

Each one taught me differently.
Gajin was harsh.
He had to be.
He knew I could do better, so he constantly called me out.
"Get closer. Cut out negative space."
Just relentlessly critical on my mistakes.

Shivam was more like an older brother.
He was kind the whole time.
"Try this angle. Try this angle."
He'd take a couple pictures for me to show how it was done.

Kevin helped me with getting the right settings occasionally.
Just dialing in the technical stuff when I was lost.

Jacob shared his stories about editing color and all these different things.
How he approaches his work.

## What I Came In Knowing (and Not Knowing)

Now going to technical details of the workshop.
When I first came in, I wish I recorded my thoughts, but when I was younger I thought I knew how to frame.
I remember doing these pictures of my parents posing together, looking at each other in love and all that stuff, with the mountains in the background.
But over the years I had to take a lot of photos for myself.
This is more quick and convenient, setting up my tripod and all that stuff.
I didn't pay attention to the background for others.
I was honestly selfishly just looking for cool spots for myself and not thinking about other people's stories and the world around me.
And for the past couple months, my friends, especially this one guy, whenever we travelled together he always asked me to take pictures for him.
He was good at taking my pictures, but I never took good pictures for him.
Up until this workshop, I also have been doing a lot more content and I've been realizing that I actually have not been doing that good.
I have been doing good content, like stories, only slowly getting better and better, but I still didn't know how to use my camera properly.
I didn't know how to do a lot of stuff properly, so there's still a lot of work to do.
And so I knew I had a lot of room to improve.

## What I Actually Learned

So like I said at first, the main skills I learned—scratch that.
The main skills I learned were framing, going up close for portraits, small technical details like exposure, all that stuff for the camera.
Paying attention to predicting what is about to happen and having intuition for what people might do.
Paying attention to light.
Going away from tourist photos and going to small, small moments.
Like sure I can get some landscape pictures to just set the background, but it's like going up close and into the action.

Another big part is making people comfortable.
Especially if I'm going for portraits, I can't be timid or whatever.
I have to smile and go up to them big.
I put my camera up of course, but be comfortable.
Maybe talk to them if they say no.
Afterwards show them the picture and say thanks and maybe continue the conversation if I can.
A lot of photography is being extremely observational about the world around me.
Not just being selfish, like I missed this one shot, or taking so many different angles of one thing.
But it's like, when I find one subject, take one super close up shot, one wider shot.
And that's it.
Just one, two, maybe three shots of different angles, but maybe one low, one high, one back.
That's it.
Don't overthink.
Find a subject.
Ideally all the technical details like framing and whatever should be intact.
So in that moment, go get it and move on.

Day 1 versus Day 10—completely different.
On day one, I took a lot more landscapes and didn't go up close to people.
Now I definitely try to go up close to people and find specific actions.
Though I've noticed I didn't take as many landscapes as I normally would.
I want to make sure I'm still getting landscapes.

Now when I scroll Instagram, I'm noticing I call out people if they have too much negative space.
If they're not angling things properly.
If they're not going up close to people.
I'm just finding a lot more mistakes or things I would do differently.

Even when I walk around, I see interesting frames.
Things that give cool perspective.
Stories I'm noticing now are when people are interacting with each other.
When people are focused on their actions.

## The Content Grind

I also am realizing like if I'm committing to content and everything, I gotta be on top of my stuff.
Like every day I gotta sit down, review my photos, do light edits, pick which ones I like and keep constantly on track of my content.
This week I did not do my daily reels or whatever.
That's fine.
It was an exhausting, tiring setup.
When I came home each day, I just wanted to brain rot.
Not think.
Just scroll social media to rest.
Now I wish I got more content out, but what are you gonna do?

But now it's like I have to catch up.
If I'm overthinking what I'm sharing, I will fall behind and I'll be completely stressed.

I still feel guilty that I haven't made any videos for like a week.
I need to get back on it.
But the pressure is real—every time you stop making content, you fall off.
People forget.
You lose that momentum.

At the same time, I need to be patient.
I need to wait for people to move.
I need to be observant of the small details that don't need to be there.
I need to be observant of a lot of things.
When people are with me trying to teach me stuff, I should be like, "Okay, cool, this is pretty interesting."
Give them my full undivided attention.

## Photography as Practice

Philosophically, I'm realizing that photography is deeply, and art in general is deeply introspective.
I need to learn to overcome my insecurities.
I need to overcome my selfishness.
I need to become a lot more aware of the world around me.

It connects to the Gita.
You have to be detached from this constant producing.
But when you commit to something, you gotta stick to it.
Don't overthink like, "Oh no, I'm not satisfied with my work," and just give up.

Photography helps me forget my own mission.
Let myself be absorbed by the rest of the world.
It shows me that other people's worlds are probably just as important as mine.
Overcoming ego by serving their stories, not mine.
Good perspective.
For art, you have to look at other people's inspiration.
When mentors criticize you, you gotta not take it personal.
Just keep learning.
Keep observing how you can make your work better.

At the same time, I now need to find better inspiration for things.
I need to look at maybe art books.
I need to look at more film.
See how it inspires me.
Be more open when I'm traveling.
Be more observant.
I think I'm already decent, but there's a lot more room I can improve.
The community itself can be fascinating.
Sure, some people care about awards and accolades, but some people are the true pursuers of passion and art.

Overall, I'm extremely grateful for this experience.
I have learned a lot, experienced a lot.
I have grown so indescribably over these past couple days, I don't even know how to express it.
And yeah, I'm just glad I went through this.
And there's still more deeper thoughts.
I still feel like an idiot compared to some of these people.
A lot more for me to grow and improve in.
But that is life, isn't it?
I should keep this an art, I should keep this a passion.
Eventually I do need to find my work, but it's completely fine if I have hobbies.
I feel like I'm realizing that in my 20s I should still be okay, at least this next year, pursuing passions.
I think perhaps my one big thing can be solidly improving my content network.
I think I can still give myself a bit more leeway to pursue interests.
And I'm glad that this was one of them.
