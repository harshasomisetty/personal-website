We think there's never any downtime per se, but as I first got there about 30 minutes earlier than I was supposed to come by three, I still became too late. Ironically the guy who left early was the first person who came and at first it was just nice talking to him. It's been that was the first time I met him in real life and then from there slowly people started trickling in.

After that I thought I'd have to do introductions for people but everyone immediately meshed. Seven of the people came from New York and they all rented the cars together so half of them already kind of knew each other and the other people immediately meshed. I'm so surprised how it worked out. Sometimes I had to butt in when I knew two people had a good background. Some people were interested in medicine but they didn't know about it or some people cared a lot about Hindu philosophy and they didn't know about it. I just cut the chase of all this small talk.

First night was pretty chill and at first I had this idea I was gonna do this presentation of everyone's birthday or my pictures of everyone throughout my life on the last day but instead I'm like wait that's actually a fantastic icebreaker. Doing a discussion for four or five hours would be too much but instead I did this presentation for two hours on the first day when everyone came and I talked about how I know them, what impact they had on my life and then what I thought about them. I had them also come up to the front of the room and they also talked about what they knew about me and immediately everyone could see my relationship with everyone and they have talking points. At first it was kind of awkward because no one knew each other but after that it added an air of love and everyone could open up a lot more and that was a pretty important thing. How do you make people open up especially when they don't know each other? That's an extremely important part of culture building.

After that, we already had food by that time. The buffet was already set by the time everyone came and everyone was just eating. We already ate and it was perfect timing. After that, it was a big board game, not board game, but we had UNO cards. We had poker. I saw everyone just chilling at the basement. Some people are talking upstairs and we just talked until I didn't sleep until 7am the first day. It was insane. So many people were just up and I didn't have to monitor everyone because what was interesting was that no one had any ego, but everyone's extremely accomplished and we're just very open to talking to whoever, learning from everyone.

Because everyone was up pretty late, we had a chill morning after. We had some breakfast food. We were supposed to go to paintball at 11 a.m. but there's just no point of rushing everyone. The idea is to be chill, just have fun. A slow brunch basically. We ate leftover food and around 1:30 we left. At that point I felt weird. What the fuck was the point of this? It feels like energy was being lost. I didn't think people were gonna get close but at some point if you have a plan you just got to stick to it, right? We got there and that's when I was talking about the motions I felt. First I realized I got scammed because I booked through the cabin people and I wasted $300 but again, what are you gonna do now? That already happened in the past. Either I could be sad that it happened or just focus on what's the next best step. The next best step is just to enjoy even though you made a mistake. Paintball was fun. That was a physical activity with everyone moving around and competitiveness and a complete new environment. When you go out of a place and come back it's like okay, more perspective. That's cool. Everyone loved it. A lot of people never did paintball. This is the first bucket list kind of item and it was just fun for everyone.

Having Vishnu there, he was 17, everyone else was 22 or older. But even everyone treated Vishnu with respect, he had a great time meeting everyone. He was the one blood relative I could trust and rely on. I relied on Amruth, Trilok, Kartik a lot, but having Vishnu there was good moral support. Hence why I need a girl. I feel like hosting stuff with a partner would make it so much nicer. But anyway, I digress.

After paintball we slowly started getting ready. Everyone was tired obviously and by that point it was perfect timing because the KBBQ guy came. While everyone's getting ready and showering, the KBBQ guy came and everyone's eating. I was a joke and around 8 o'clock I wanted to do a campfire discussion. I got everyone over, we built this campfire and then we basically did a Socratic seminar about what was important to us. I started going in like okay, I traveled, I did all this, but it's never enough. There's also some chase. I did this two-minute silent meditation where I just had everyone think about what was important to them. I basically led the Socratic seminar where I had almost everyone talk about what was important. We came to the realization that friendship, love, community, and sharing life with other people, that's the most important goal.

After that, I also asked this question: if you had $10 million but you didn't have to really work, what would you do for the next five years? This was actually the first question I asked, oh what do you think would be valuable? But we got diverted into the friendship thing. Really enough, I felt a lot of pressure as a moderator. At one point, I don't think I was fully intent listening to everyone. I was just trying to make sure everyone felt heard. I was watching people's body language to see if they're uncomfortable or they felt like they wanted to say something so I could call them out. I guess that was a good learning lesson. It is important, but I didn't have to overthink and just be so strict with my moderation, just be natural. But I think it's so useful because everyone had a chance to literally talk, right? After that, Kartik and Trilok helped steer the conversation back to that important question because then we went around the circle, everyone literally talked about what they would want to spend their life on. Some of their answers were interesting. One guy wanted to be a life coach. One guy wanted to be... I don't know, just really, really interesting. All these accomplished people talking about what exactly they want. But when you're hosting it's...

After the Socratic Seminar, the Fireplace discussion, a lot of people told me that they were concerned about if it would be awkward or not. After that everyone had such positive reception to it, getting that many people to open up. I'm proud of myself for this. I had to be vulnerable first and set the stage for everyone else, right? But once that happened everyone loved it. It takes sacrifice and being willing to be uncomfortable in certain situations. But if you're able to set the scene, remember how we did the dance circle? It takes a lot of mental effort to do the first thing, but once it does then it just creates joy for everyone. That was an interesting learning experience there.

Eventually every single person will hit every single goal. What's important is as you learn, you're also sharing what you learn to other people. I taught Vishnu so much stuff by forcing him to learn. He's extremely grateful for that. And I know, I hope I have positively impacted his life. But in general, I think there's just so much more about the world that I just need to explore. I can't die without understanding how different people live, why people think a certain way, even their languages, their culture. I think I would just want to dedicate my life to learning, but also sharing what I learned to other people.

After campfire, it was cold and we went in but it was just so natural. Everyone now opened up, everyone's comfortable talking to each other. Some people went upstairs, some people went down and again, I stayed up until two. I took a quick power nap for an hour but then my friend and I talked from three to seven and I stayed up for another hour walking around, slightly meditating. Now I was winding down. It was the last day. We woke up kind of late, around 11 and slowly talking as we packed up. The checkout was 11 but we extended to fucking four. But having that, another important thing about events is when you know you have interesting events, mini events, you need to have a lot of buffer time. There's no point of rushing people. That rush destroys a lot of the emotional buildup that's there. If you want people to be comfortable, you can't also rush them, right? At the end we did a final group picture inside the cabin and that was it. Afterwards we came up with a spontaneous idea to go play Topgolf in Edison. The Topgolf was a bit too energy intensive but they all went to this restaurant. I was gonna go but then I forgot my fucking laptop. I drove two hours from Pennsylvania to Jersey and then I realized I forgot it and I drove back two hours and then another four hours. To be honest, I almost fucking crashed my car at one point. I was so delirious. I was already on three hours of sleep. I almost swore I was two feet away from hitting something. I didn't tell anyone else this but fuck, life flashed before my eyes. Was there a car still, have everyone in a car? There was plenty of car space. No worries there.

Knowing how different people vibe, knowing how open they are. I also have done good exercise in getting people to open up. I've learned about how to do proper surprises. There is so much that goes into hosting good events. I guess even at a high level when I can get helpers to do it, I still need to make sure I learn everything myself as well. I'm surprised at who turned up and who showed up versus who did not. I'm surprised at the mindsets that some people have. I have been really growing a lot in my experience.
