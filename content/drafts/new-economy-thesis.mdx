There is a reason why kids wanting to be astronauts has changed to kids wanting to become content creators. The younger generation suddenly understand the power of content and how, you know in the past, capitalism was the reigning source of the economic engine where money begets money. There is now an interesting pattern of content and capital becoming invertible.

But, invertibility only exists when the function is based on Dharma. This piece will go indepth into my take.

Capital can be turned into content. You can hire people to make your content. You can pay for ads to get views. You immediately make it into content. This is a given because by definition these companies exist because they're marketplace for content and on top of that there are companies that pay, businesses pay to advertise.

Now content can also be turned into capital. If you make enough content and you make a personal brand, people will pay you in brand deals. You can raise attention for your project and raise money. You can find talent, add revenue, whatever. This is not something that needs to be proved because it is a given. You see content creators, the whole market existing because it's possible.

But what is the end goal here? The properly done system actually propagates itself. If capital is used wisely, it makes viral content, but the viral content brings more capital back. And if content is done with pure intentions, it brings in capital, which again makes the content.

I actually think the biggest problem that most people face is loneliness. To be honest, we're all pretty lucky that we have some people in our lives who are similar to us. I know for a fact, I am extremely lucky that the fact that 20 people gave their weekends to just spend time with me. I don't know what kind of good luck I have, but yeah. ### Lack of purpose and mentors

A lot of people don't even know what to spend their time on. Society tells them that you need to make money but you don't know what makes money until you just get good at something. To be honest, when I was getting into programming I knew I'd make money but I was just obsessed with the side projects I could do. And eventually I just got so good that people started paying me. This happens in anything. If you're pursuing music, at first you're just messing around on the DJ board but suddenly eventually you get so good that people are paying you to do it.

What your skills are good at is non-obvious and when people are increasingly lonely, lack connections, and are sucked into this capitalist system, it's hard to develop any skills.

And a lot of people do want to learn things. Like for example, I've been wanting to learn guitar for more than a decade. I've had teachers on and off, but with the busy life schedule, it's hard to really stay track to something. It's hard to just have someone who kind of just pokes you every single day and just makes that brainless possible to learn anything. That kind of consistency is extremely hard. Most people have to dedicate literally 10 hours a day to something to really get good at it. Maybe you can practice something for an hour a day, but eventually if you don't see progress, if you don't have like those IRL guidelines, you know, it's just hard to sustain it. Even if it is, even if it is just a will issue and people should be able to sustain it, it's hard. People would unanimously want to get better at guitar or painting or whatever, whatever.
